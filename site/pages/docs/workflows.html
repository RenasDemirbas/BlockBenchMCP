Workflows

The order that works for each kind of model, and what to ask for at each step. Building in steps and checking a screenshot after each one is faster than asking for everything at once.

Minecraft-style entity

Cube models in the Bedrock entity (or Java, or generic) format, animated through group bones.

  1. Project and skeleton. create_project, then the bone tree with add_groups: body › head, body › leg_fl and so on. Animations move these groups, so build them first.
  2. Geometry. add_cubes into the groups. mirror: true creates the opposite limb and renames it left↔right. add_planes for fur and leaf cards.
  3. Check. capture_multi_view from the front, side and top. Fix proportions now; moving geometry after painting breaks the UVs.
  4. Texture. generate_texture_template first, then paint_faces and paint_texture. Without a template many faces share one UV area and painting one repaints the others.
  5. Animate. create_animation, set_keyframes, and mirror_keyframes with a half-cycle phase_offset for walk cycles. validate_model then finds parts that intersect or float loose during the animation; query_geometry checks that feet touch the ground.
  6. Export. project_file with export (bedrock_geo) and export_animations.
Make a wolf in the Bedrock entity format. Build only the skeleton and the shape in grey cubes first and show me three views. If I like it, texture it and add a walk cycle.

Low-poly, hand-painted character

PS1-style characters with painted textures, built from meshes in the generic free format.

  1. Reference. compare_reference against your picture from the same angle. It reports silhouette overlap (IoU) and, for each height band, how many units wider or narrower the model is.
  2. Blockout. add_loft builds arms, legs, trousers and torso from rings: give each ring a centre and a size and you get a tapering, bending limb. A cylinder from add_mesh_primitive makes a hat brim; a plane and edit_mesh solidify make a cape.
  3. Shape. transform_mesh tapers, bends and twists; add falloff so a pulled nose or a bent knee drags the surface around it. edit_mesh extrudes, insets, loop-cuts and bevels, with select: "previous" chaining steps in one call. Run compare_reference again after each change.
  4. Unwrap. unwrap_mesh with pixel_density 32–64 and density_scale: {"head": 2}. Limbs come out as single strips; existing paint moves to the new layout.
  5. Flat colours. palette ramp for colour ramps, paint_faces to fill. On meshes, faces: ["up"] picks the faces that point up. project_reference can paint the reference picture on as a starting point.
  6. Blend and bake. blend_seams softens the colour breaks where parts meet; bake_texture with layer: "shading" paints light, ambient occlusion and edge highlights as hue-shifted pixel-art steps on their own layer.
  7. Clean up. palette quantize snaps stray colours to the palette.
Record the build: record_build start at the beginning turns every edit into a frame, and stop writes a part-by-part GIF.

Skinned mesh with an armature

One continuous mesh that bends at the joints, such as a tentacle, a tail, a cape or a low-poly character. Use groups instead for blocky rigs where parts move rigidly.

  1. Build the mesh in the free format.
  2. add_armature with bones given as head and tail points and meshes to attach. It skins them in the same call: Blockbench's own solver runs first, then any vertex it leaves out or hands to a bone far away is re-solved by distance.
  3. capture_screenshot with view_mode: "weighted_bone_colors" shows which bone owns which vertex.
  4. vertex_weights smooth with near: {bone, distance} softens a joint that creases; set paints a weight onto a selection; inspect lists vertices that follow no bone.
  5. Pose the bones with set_keyframes, or drive a chain with add_ik_controllers (target and source are bone names).
  6. Export with project_file export glb and options: {armature: true}.

Pixel-art sprites for a 2D game

pixel_art draws the model by pixel-art rules: scale snapped to texture pixels, no anti-aliasing, a few shading bands and a 1 px outline. It is not a shrunk screenshot.

  • Views: side, front, back, top, three_quarter, top_down, isometric (2:1), true_isometric, or a free yaw/pitch.
  • Directions: directions: 8 for a rotation set; mirror_directions: true halves the work on symmetric models.
  • Style and palette: outlined, clean, minecraft, flat; the model's own colours or pico8, sweetie16, endesga32, db32, with optional Bayer dithering.
  • Sheets: export_sheet writes an animation as one PNG plus Aseprite JSON, with scale and the feet pivot fixed once so frames don't drift.

Tips that save a round trip

  • Paint whole limbs: target: {element: "leg_fl", faces: "all"} covers every cube in a group; "*" is the whole model.
  • Gradients with space: "world" follow the model's height instead of restarting on every cube.
  • Keyframe values can be Molang, like "math.sin(query.anim_time*360)*15". Times snap to the animation's FPS grid; snap: false keeps exact times.
  • Bones with a rest rotation add keyframe values on top of it; they are not absolute angles.
  • Bake IK with bake_ik_animation before exporting to Bedrock or Java, which have no IK.
  • A model that looks washed out or covered in a grid is usually Blockbench's brightness or pixel_grid setting, not the texture.
